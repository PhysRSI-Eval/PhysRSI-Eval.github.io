Motivation

Code-as-Policy agents now improve themselves in the physical world

Code-as-Policy agents drive embodied tasks with self-written control code instead of direct action outputs. They have grown from few-shot programs over hand-built primitives into agents that write, execute and revise their own control code from graded outcomes — yet a self-written fix can work once and fail on the repeat.

Physical RSI

A frozen model revises its own code, tools and notes from graded physical outcomes, without weight updates.

Existing evaluations cannot tell where this capability comes from. Embodied benchmarks report endpoint success over independent attempts or wall-clock budgets, which conflates search with improvement: at a constant per-attempt success rate of 0.1, 17 attempts already give an 83% chance of at least one success. Self-evolution benchmarks that do measure improvement live in digital domains without a physical outcome verifier.

Three questions

What we ask

Q1 · Capability

Which capabilities drive physical RSI, and how can they be quantified?

Q2 · Prediction

Can VLM capabilities predict physical RSI — its relative ranking across agents and its trend over rounds?

Q3 · Generalization

Do improved policies generalize beyond the scenes they were refined on?

The benchmark

PhysRSI-Eval: a fixed budget of judged physical attempts

Each task starts with one zero-shot round (K0). The agent then gets at most 17 self-improvement rounds (K1–K17), revising a persistent workspace of skills, tools, policy and memory after every graded attempt, and stops at its first success. Every agent faces the same budget.

Findings

What we find

Finding 1

Physical Barrel Theory

Strong VLM agents are good in many areas — coding, planning, forecasting — yet some of them stay near zero success on embodied tasks across every round.

The shortest stave

As a barrel holds water only up to its shortest stave, each dimension of embodied performance (e.g. success rate, cost) hinges on one critical VLM ability. If this ability falls below a minimum requirement, the other VLM abilities can hardly compensate.

Finding 2

Physical RSI is predictable

Rather than exact scores, we predict two coarser quantities. Public VLM benchmark scores predict the relative ranking of agents on PhysRSI-Eval, and physical RSI follows a predictable scaling curve over self-improvement rounds, resembling the scaling laws of LLMs: later rounds can be anticipated from earlier ones.

Finding 3

Gains reflect improvement; transfer is limited

Wiping the workspace every round collapses late-round success, so the gains are not resampling. Yet part of the gain is scene memorization.

Contributions

In short

  1. Benchmark. PhysRSI-Eval with a five-axis profile of physical RSI under a fixed trial budget.
  2. Physical Barrel Theory. Evidence across eight agents that the weakest critical ability caps physical RSI.
  3. Prediction. VLM capability predicts the relative ranking of physical RSI, and physical RSI follows a predictable scaling curve over self-improvement rounds.
  4. Generalization. Ablation and held-out tests of whether improved policies transfer.
